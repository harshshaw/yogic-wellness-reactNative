## Before You Begin — Consult a Doctor If You:

- Are **pregnant** or postpartum
- Have a **heart condition**, arrhythmia, or history of cardiovascular disease
- Have **high or low blood pressure**
- Have a **respiratory condition** such as asthma, COPD, or recent respiratory infection
- Have or have had **epilepsy, seizures, or fainting episodes** (syncope)
- Have **glaucoma or detached retina**
- Have had **recent surgery** (especially abdominal, chest, or eye surgery)
- Have a **hernia** or acute abdominal condition
- Have **panic disorder or severe anxiety** — some breath retention practices can
  trigger symptoms
- Are under the influence of alcohol, sedatives, or other impairing substances

## Universal Safety Rules (All Techniques)

1. **Stop immediately** if you feel dizzy, light-headed, tingly, short of breath,
   nauseous, or experience chest pain or a racing heart. Return to normal breathing.
2. Practice **seated or lying down** in a safe, stable position — never while
   standing in an unsafe place.
3. **Never practice while driving**, operating machinery, swimming, or in water.
4. Breathe **gently and without strain**. Breath work should never feel forced or
   painful. Mild effort is normal; discomfort is a signal to stop.
5. Practice on a relatively **empty stomach** (wait 2–3 hours after a heavy meal).
6. Children should practice only with adult supervision, and only gentle techniques.
7. Progress gradually — do not exceed the recommended rounds or durations,
   especially as a beginner.

## Technique-Specific Cautions

### 4-7-8 Breathing (Deep Breathing / Relaxing Breath)
*Inhale 4s · Hold 7s (Kumbhaka) · Exhale 8s*

- The **7-second breath hold** can cause light-headedness in beginners. If holding
  feels stressful, shorten the hold or skip it entirely and simply lengthen the exhale.
- Limit to **4 rounds per session** when starting out (the app's default).
- Not recommended without medical guidance if you have severe respiratory illness,
  uncontrolled blood pressure, or are pregnant — breath retention alters blood
  pressure and CO₂ levels.

### Box Breathing (4-4-4-4 / Sama Vritti)
*Inhale 4s · Hold 4s · Exhale 4s · Hold 4s*

- Involves **two breath holds per cycle** — the same retention cautions as 4-7-8
  apply (blood pressure, pregnancy, cardiovascular conditions).
- If the empty-lung hold (after exhale) causes anxiety or air hunger, skip it and
  practice simple equal breathing (inhale 4, exhale 4) instead.

### Nadi Shodhana (Alternate Nostril Breathing / Focus & Clarity)
*Alternating nostrils with gentle finger pressure*

- Generally one of the **gentlest** techniques, but avoid if you have a **blocked
  nose, sinus infection, cold, or flu** — forcing airflow through a congested
  nostril causes strain.
- Use **light finger pressure** only; never block both nostrils at once.
- Keep the breath silent and smooth — audible effort means you are forcing it.

### Kapalbhati (Breath of Fire / Skull-Shining Breath)
*Rapid, forceful exhales with passive inhales*

- This is the **most intense technique in the app** and is classified as an
  energizing, advanced practice. Beginners should learn it slowly and gently.
- **Do not practice** if you are pregnant or menstruating, or if you have heart
  disease, high blood pressure, hernia, epilepsy, vertigo, glaucoma, recent
  abdominal surgery, or acute anxiety/panic — the rapid abdominal pumping and
  hyperventilation-like pattern significantly affects blood pressure and CO₂ levels.
- Dizziness or tingling is a sign you are over-breathing: **stop and rest**.
- Always practice on an **empty stomach**.

### Anxiety Reset (Gentle Guided Breathing)

- Designed to be the safest, gentlest session — slow breathing with no forceful
  retention. Still, if slowing the breath ever *increases* your anxiety (common in
  panic disorder), pause, breathe naturally, and try again later or consult a
  mental-health professional.

## Mental Health Note

Breathing practices can bring up strong emotions or, rarely, feelings of panic or
dissociation. This is a signal to stop and ground yourself. Karmana's AI Companion
is a supportive tool, **not a therapist or crisis service**. If you are in
emotional distress or crisis, contact a mental-health professional or your local
emergency/crisis helpline immediately.
